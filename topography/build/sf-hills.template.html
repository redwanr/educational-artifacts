The sheet is a rectangle of elevation data, not the city. Below the dashed line it keeps going into San Mateo County, far enough to hold the whole of San Bruno Mountain.

Past the county line

San Bruno Mountain closes the bottom of the sheet: a four-mile fault-block ridge in San Mateo County, and the southern horizon seen from downtown. Its Radio Peak is 1,319 ft — higher than anything inside San Francisco, and 390 ft above Mount Davidson.

It carries a hollow dot and is left out of the roster and the count. Click its name to read it like any other summit.

Reading the rings

Crowded → steep. Filbert Street's stairs, or the quarried face of Telegraph Hill.
Spread wide → gentle. The Sunset's sand sheet barely climbs at all.
Closed rings → a summit. The innermost ring is the top.
A pinched figure-eight → a saddle: the low pass between two summits, like the gap between the Twin Peaks.

Each height is read off the same grid the contours are drawn from, so the number and the rings always agree. San Francisco's high ground is one connected massif in the middle of the city — Twin Peaks, Sutro, Davidson, Forest Hill — while the famous downtown hills (Nob, Russian, Telegraph) are barely a third as tall. Hover any row to find that hill on the map.
